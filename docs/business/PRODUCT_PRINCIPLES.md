# LifeCircle OS — Product Principles

*Version 1.0 | Owner: CEO + CPO | This document settles arguments.*

These are not aspirational goals. They are constitutional laws. If a feature, design decision, or notification exists that violates any principle below, it must be rejected — regardless of how clever or well-intentioned it is.

---

## 1. We Reduce Anxiety. We Do Not Increase Engagement.
Our success metric is how little our users need to think about the app, not how often they open it. A family that opens LifeCircle once a week and feels complete peace of mind is a success. A family that checks it nervously five times a day is a failure.

## 2. We Never Create Guilt.
Every design decision must be evaluated against this question: *"Could this make someone feel judged, inadequate, or ashamed?"* Guilt is not a feature. Shame is not a mechanism.

## 3. Every Notification Must Justify Its Existence.
The default answer for any proposed notification is **No**. The burden of proof lies with the feature, not with silence. If a notification cannot articulate exactly how it reduces a family's mental load, it does not ship.

## 4. Privacy Is a Product Feature, Not a Compliance Checkbox.
When we say *"Your data is yours,"* we mean it architecturally. The cloud coordinates. The device understands. Families own their truth. We will never sell, monetize, or use family behavior data to drive engagement or advertising.

## 5. Simplicity Beats Feature Count.
We will always choose to make one thing work beautifully over adding ten things that work adequately. A smaller, calmer, more trustworthy product is worth more than a sprawling, anxious one.

## 6. The Device Understands. The Cloud Coordinates.
Business logic, confidence scoring, and peace calculations always live on the device. The cloud is a trusted courier, not an intelligence layer. This is a permanent architectural commitment, not a temporary decision.

## 7. Trust Compounds. Betrayal Compounds Faster.
Every interaction either builds or erodes trust. Our users are entrusting us with information about their families' health, finances, and care responsibilities. We treat that data accordingly — with the same gravity a trusted family member would.

## 8. Accessible by Default.
Every feature must be usable by an 80-year-old parent or grandparent who is not digitally native. Accessibility is not an accommodation. It is the baseline standard of design.

## 9. Evidence Outweighs Opinions.
No roadmap item enters implementation because it is interesting. Every new feature must satisfy the Rule of Three: independently validated by three separate families. *"I think users would love this"* is not sufficient.

## 10. We Build for Decades, Not for Downloads.
Every technical and product decision must be evaluated against a 10-year horizon. We will choose boring, reliable, and maintainable over novel, clever, and impressive.
