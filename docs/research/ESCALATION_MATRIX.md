# Research Escalation Matrix

**Version:** 1.0
**Owner:** Research Director
**Scope:** Actions to take if a participant discloses severe distress or a dangerous situation during a research session.

## 1. Objective
To protect the well-being of the participant and legally protect the research team. Our product touches the lives of families, which can sometimes surface sensitive or volatile family dynamics (e.g., medical negligence, financial abuse, domestic distress).

## 2. Immediate Actions During Session

If a participant discloses a situation that indicates **immediate physical danger, abuse, or severe medical negligence**:

1. **Pause the Session:** Do not probe for more details. Say: *"I want to acknowledge what you just shared. That sounds incredibly difficult. I want to respect your privacy, so we can pause or end the session here if you'd like."*
2. **Do Not Counsel:** You are a researcher, not a therapist or social worker. Do not offer advice, solutions, or promises of help.
3. **Offer to Stop Recording:** Immediately ask if they would like the recording stopped and deleted.
4. **End the Session Early (If Appropriate):** If the participant is highly distressed, conclude the interview gracefully. 

## 3. Post-Session Escalation Path

**Within 1 Hour of Session End:**
1. **Document the Event:** Write a factual, objective summary in a separate, secure file (DO NOT put sensitive disclosures in the standard `OBSERVATION_TEMPLATE.md`). Use only the Family ID (e.g., F001), no names.
2. **Notify Legal/Privacy Officer:** Immediately escalate the objective summary to the Legal and Privacy Officers.
3. **Determine Reporting Requirements:** Legal will determine if the disclosure mandates reporting to local authorities (e.g., elder abuse reporting laws in the participant's jurisdiction).
4. **Scrub Engineering Data:** Ensure no trace of the sensitive disclosure makes it into the `DECISION_LOG.md` or engineering roadmap.
